5 HIPAA-Compliant Calendly Alternatives for US Clinics (2026)

By Nexyra Studio · November 19, 2026 · 10 min read

Short answer: if your clinic books patients, you need a scheduler that will sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) — and Calendly will not, on any plan. The closest like-for-like replacement is Acuity Scheduling at $49 a month (annual billing). Therapists usually fit SimplePractice better, small practices with heavy intake fit IntakeQ, and dental or medical practices should look at NexHealth or Weave.

This guide covers the five strongest HIPAA-compliant Calendly alternatives for US clinics: what each one costs, which plan the BAA actually sits on, and who each one fits — plus the mistakes that catch practices out when they switch.

Why Calendly doesn’t work for patient bookings

Calendly is a good general scheduler. It is not a healthcare scheduler. The problem is one document: the Business Associate Agreement.

Under HIPAA, any vendor that stores or handles protected health information (PHI) on your behalf must sign a BAA with you before the first patient books — and a patient booking with a name, a time, and a reason for the visit is PHI. Calendly signs no BAA on any plan: not Free, not Standard, not Teams, and not Enterprise. Its own terms say customer data must not contain PHI, and its security page lists SOC 2, ISO 27001, and PCI certifications but no HIPAA program. No setting changes that, and no higher tier unlocks it.

That is the whole story: the product was never designed for patient data, and the company has said so plainly. We covered this in detail in our guide to whether Calendly is HIPAA compliant. The short version: if a tool will not sign a BAA, patient data does not belong in it — at any price.

What makes a scheduler HIPAA-compliant?

HIPAA does not certify software — there is no government “HIPAA certified” badge, and no vendor can show you one. What a scheduler can do is give you the pieces your compliance depends on:

  • A signed BAA. This is the one non-negotiable. The BAA defines how the vendor handles PHI on your behalf — what it can use it for, how it protects it, and what happens in a breach. Get it signed before the first patient books, and check which plan it sits on: on several tools below, the BAA is only on a higher tier, so the price of the tool is the price of that tier.
  • Encryption in transit and at rest. Standard on any serious tool; ask anyway, and ask what happens to your data if you leave.
  • Access controls. Not everyone on staff needs to see every patient’s record. Role-based access matters more as the team grows — a front-desk login should not see the same thing a provider sees.
  • Audit logs. A record of who viewed or changed what, and when. You will not look at it most days, but it is the first thing that matters if something goes wrong.
  • Secure reminders and forms. A reminder text that names a procedure or a condition carries a different risk from one that says only the time and the place. Intake forms should travel through the platform’s secure forms, not by email or an unencrypted web form.

A BAA is one part of compliance, not the whole of it — your workflow is the rest. And a vendor that markets “HIPAA compliant” without a signed BAA is selling you a word, not a protection.

The 5 alternatives

1. Acuity Scheduling — the closest Calendly replacement

Acuity does the same core job as Calendly — a booking page connected to your calendar, with intake forms, payments, and reminders — and it is the only general scheduler on this list that signs a BAA. That makes it the most direct swap for a clinic that likes Calendly’s way of working but needs the BAA.

HIPAA / BAA: Yes — but only on the Premium plan (also called Powerhouse). The Starter ($16/mo) and Standard ($27/mo) plans do not offer a BAA, so price Acuity at the Premium tier from the start if you handle patient data.

Price (Nov 2026): Premium is $49 a month billed annually ($61 month-to-month), flat — not per seat — for up to 36 calendars. SMS reminders start on the $27 Standard tier; API access and removing Acuity’s branding need Premium.

Best for: Solo and small practices that want a Calendly-style booking page with a BAA, without buying a full practice-management system.

Honest limitations: Acuity is a scheduler, not a practice-management system — no charting, no insurance billing, no EHR. And turning on HIPAA mode switches off some features, including Reserve with Google and Outlook/iCloud calendar sync — a real trade-off if your patients find you through Google booking.

2. SimplePractice — for therapists and mental-health practices

SimplePractice is a practice-management platform built for therapists, counsellors, and other mental-health professionals: scheduling, clinical notes, telehealth, billing, and a client portal in one system, with a BAA on every plan.

HIPAA / BAA: Yes, on every plan. The BAA takes effect when your account is created — no upgrade needed, no separate request. SimplePractice is also HITRUST certified.

Price (Nov 2026): Starter $49 a month, Essential $79 a month, Plus $99 a month, per clinician. Full appointment reminders (email, text, and voice) need Essential; Starter sends telehealth reminders only. On Plus, each additional clinician is $74 a month. Electronic insurance claims are $0.39 each on every plan.

Best for: Solo and small therapy practices that want scheduling, notes, telehealth, and billing under one BAA instead of four separate tools.

Honest limitations: It is built for therapy and behavioural health — a dental or medical practice needing PMS sync or insurance verification will not find it here. And the per-clinician pricing adds up: two clinicians on Plus pay $173 a month, not $99.

3. IntakeQ — for small practices where intake is half the job

IntakeQ started as an intake-form builder for health practices and grew into a practice-management platform (sold as PracticeQ): secure forms, e-signatures, scheduling, a booking widget, a client portal, and payments, with a BAA on every plan.

HIPAA / BAA: Yes, on every plan — including the low-volume tiers. The admin signs the BAA inside the account. Check that it is signed before you send the first form.

Price (Nov 2026): Scheduling is only in the Practice Management plans, not the Forms Only plans. Practice Management starts at $59.90 a month (low-volume, capped at 25 appointments per practitioner per 30 days) or $84.90 a month for the full plan, plus $25–$30 per additional practitioner. Forms Only — no scheduling — starts at $29.90 a month (low-volume) or $54.90 a month.

Best for: Small practices where intake forms are as important as the booking itself — therapy, allied health, wellness, and cash-pay practices that want forms, signatures, and scheduling under one BAA.

Honest limitations: The low-volume caps are real: 25 appointments a month per practitioner is not much for a busy practice, and the full plan is the honest price for most clinics. And IntakeQ is not a PMS — a dental practice needing real-time sync should look at NexHealth or Weave instead.

4. NexHealth — for dental and medical practices that need real-time sync

NexHealth is a patient-experience platform for dental and medical practices: online scheduling, reminders, forms, reviews, and payments, built around real-time, two-way sync with practice-management systems like Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental. A patient books online and the appointment appears in your PMS immediately; time blocked in the PMS disappears online at once.

HIPAA / BAA: Yes. NexHealth signs a BAA — it is built for healthcare practices that handle PHI every day.

Price (Nov 2026): Quote-only; NexHealth publishes no price. Practices report paying from about $350 a month, with the total climbing as you add modules, plus a reported one-time implementation fee. Get the full twelve-month total in a written quote before you budget.

Best for: Dental and medical practices where the sync is the point — the schedule in the PMS and the schedule online must be the same schedule, in real time. See our dental booking guide for how the three routes compare.

Honest limitations: The price is the limitation: at a reported $350 a month and up, NexHealth is a platform purchase, not a scheduler purchase — and the modular pricing means the number in the demo is rarely the number on the first invoice.

5. Weave — for practices that want phones, texting, and scheduling in one contract

Weave bundles VoIP phones, two-way texting, appointment reminders, online scheduling, payments, and review requests into one front-office platform for dental, medical, veterinary, and optometry practices, with direct integrations into Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, and others.

HIPAA / BAA: Yes. Weave is built for healthcare practices and signs a BAA.

Price (Nov 2026): Published pricing starts at $199 a month, with three tiers (Pro, Elite, Ultimate) priced by quote. Practices commonly report paying $400–$700 a month for the tiers they actually use, plus a reported ~$750 one-time setup fee. The $199 figure is a starting price, not the price most practices pay.

Best for: Practices that want to replace a phone system and a scheduler at the same time — phones, texting, reminders, and scheduling in one contract, with patient details on screen when the phone rings.

Honest limitations: You are buying a bundle: if you only need scheduling, Weave is more tool — and more money — than the job requires. And the quote-only tiers mean you cannot compare the real price without a sales call.

How to choose

If you are a solo therapist or counsellor: SimplePractice Starter ($49/mo) and Acuity Premium ($49/mo) cost the same. Pick SimplePractice if you need notes, telehealth, and billing under one BAA; pick Acuity if you only need scheduling and want the simplest possible booking page.

If intake forms are half your workflow: IntakeQ’s Practice Management plan ($59.90 low-volume / $84.90 full) puts forms, e-signatures, and scheduling under one BAA. Just check the low-volume caps against your real appointment count before you pick the cheaper tier — 25 appointments a month per practitioner is easy to outgrow.

If you run a dental practice: the decision is about the PMS, not the scheduler. NexHealth is the pick when real-time, two-way sync with Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental is the priority; Weave is the pick when you also want to replace your phones. A three-provider practice comparing the two should budget on reported figures and get both in writing. Our dental booking guide walks through all three routes, including the scheduling your PMS already offers.

If you run a med spa: two tools from the salon world sign BAAs and are worth a look — Mangomint ($120 a month per location plus $10 per user, BAA on request) and GlossGenius (free HIPAA add-on with a BAA from the Gold plan, $48 a month billed annually). Both are built for beauty and wellness businesses rather than medical practices, so check the fit — especially around charting and medical-director sign-off — before you commit.

If you are multi-location or your booking rules do not fit any tool: that is the point where a custom build enters the conversation — see below.

When a custom build makes sense

Every tool above is a compromise: you get their booking flow, their pricing, and their feature set. A custom booking system is the alternative when the compromise costs more than the build — booking rules the platforms cannot express, a multi-location setup where per-location fees add up, or a practice that wants to own the booking experience and the patient data.

Two honest cautions. First, a custom build is not automatically HIPAA-compliant — compliance still needs the right hosting, a signed BAA with whoever hosts patient data, access controls, and audit trails. What a custom build gives you is control over all of that, instead of a vendor’s defaults. Second, for most single-location practices, one of the five tools above is cheaper and faster — and we will say so on the call.

Our custom vs. off-the-shelf framework walks through the decision in detail: the crossover point is roughly $150–$200 a month in total SaaS cost and climbing. If your total booking bill is already past that and still rising with every hire, get a custom-build quote and compare it honestly. Custom builds are quoted per project after a free call, with an ongoing monthly plan covering hosting, security, monitoring, updates, integrations, and support.

Five mistakes to avoid when switching

  1. Assuming the BAA covers every tier. On Acuity the BAA is Premium-only; on GlossGenius the free HIPAA add-on starts at Gold. The price of the tool is the price of the tier the BAA sits on — not the cheapest tier on the pricing page.
  2. Putting patient data in before the BAA is signed. On Mangomint, HIPAA mode stays off until you contact support and sign the BAA; on IntakeQ, the admin signs it inside the account. Until it is signed, the tool holds PHI under no agreement — and a missing BAA is itself a problem, not just a missing formality.
  3. Trusting “HIPAA compliant” in the marketing. There is no government “HIPAA certified” badge. Ask for the signed BAA itself, and check what it covers — some BAAs exclude specific features, add-ons, or trial accounts, and the exclusions are rarely in the sales deck.
  4. Forgetting that reminders and forms carry PHI too. A reminder that names a procedure is a different risk from one that says only the time and the place. Keep reminder texts to the time, the place, and a link — and send intake forms through the platform’s secure forms, not by email.
  5. Budgeting on the headline price. Weave’s $199 starting price and the $400–$700 practices report paying are different numbers, and NexHealth’s modules add up. Get the full twelve-month total in writing before you sign.

What it costs

  • The cheapest real options are Acuity Premium and SimplePractice Starter, both $49 a month — Acuity flat for up to 36 calendars, SimplePractice per clinician. IntakeQ’s Practice Management starts at $59.90 a month on the low-volume tier.
  • The mid-range is $79–$170 a month: SimplePractice Essential ($79) or Plus ($99), IntakeQ’s full Practice Management plan ($84.90), Mangomint for a small med spa ($130 a month).
  • The platforms are $199 a month and up: Weave publishes a $199 starting price with practices reporting $400–$700, and NexHealth is quote-only with practices reporting from about $350 a month.
  • A custom booking system has an upfront build cost and an ongoing monthly plan instead of a subscription, and is quoted per project after a free call.

Whatever you choose, get the full twelve-month total in writing before you sign: subscription, setup, add-ons, and the contract term. Several of these vendors sell on annual contracts with auto-renewal, and the headline monthly price is rarely the whole bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Calendly HIPAA compliant?

No — on any plan. Calendly signs no Business Associate Agreement on Free, Standard, Teams, or Enterprise, and its terms say customer data must not contain protected health information. Our guide to whether Calendly is HIPAA compliant explains the BAA question in detail.

What is the cheapest HIPAA-compliant scheduling software?

For a solo practice, Acuity Scheduling’s Premium plan and SimplePractice’s Starter plan both start at $49 a month — Acuity flat for up to 36 calendars, SimplePractice per clinician. Acuity stays $49 as you add calendars; SimplePractice charges per clinician. IntakeQ’s Practice Management low-volume plan starts at $59.90 a month. Whichever you pick, confirm the BAA is on the tier you are actually buying.

Does Acuity Scheduling sign a BAA?

Yes — on the Premium plan (also called Powerhouse), $49 a month billed annually or $61 month-to-month. The Starter ($16) and Standard ($27) plans do not offer a BAA. Note that turning on HIPAA mode switches off some features, including Reserve with Google and Outlook/iCloud calendar sync.

Is SimplePractice HIPAA compliant?

Yes. SimplePractice signs a Business Associate Agreement on every plan — the BAA takes effect when your account is created — and the platform is HITRUST certified. It is built for therapists and mental-health practices, with scheduling, notes, telehealth, and billing in one system.

Can I use Calendly for patient bookings if I don’t collect health details?

We would not recommend it. An appointment with a patient’s name, time, and provider can itself be protected health information, and Calendly’s terms prohibit PHI on every plan. A tool that signs a BAA removes the question entirely — Acuity’s Premium plan does the same job for $49 a month.

Is a custom booking system HIPAA compliant?

Not automatically. HIPAA compliance depends on hosting, access controls, audit trails, and a signed BAA with whoever hosts patient data. A custom build gives you control over all of that, but the work still has to be done. Our custom vs. off-the-shelf guide explains when building makes sense.

Final Takeaway

The BAA is the whole decision. Calendly will not sign one, so patient bookings need a different tool — and the right one depends on what kind of practice you run. Acuity is the closest Calendly replacement at $49 a month; SimplePractice fits therapists who want notes and billing in the same system; IntakeQ fits small practices where intake forms matter as much as booking; NexHealth and Weave fit dental and medical practices that need their PMS and their phones in the picture. Check which tier the BAA sits on, get the twelve-month total in writing, and pick the tool that fits how your practice actually works.

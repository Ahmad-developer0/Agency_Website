How to Set Up Online Booking for a Dental Practice (2026 Guide)

By Nexyra Studio · November 16, 2026 · 10 min read

Short answer: start with the scheduling your practice management system already offers, and add a dedicated booking tool only if the patient experience or the sync is not good enough. The one rule that matters most is that online bookings must land directly on your real schedule — in Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental — the moment the patient books. Anything that creates a second calendar, or a request someone has to confirm by phone, is not online booking. It is a web form with extra steps.

This guide walks through how to set up online booking for a dental practice: what patients expect, the three routes, the setup steps, the HIPAA points, and what each option costs.

What patients expect from dental booking

  • Real open times, not a request form. A form that says “we will call you to confirm” is the experience patients are trying to escape.
  • Booking that works on a phone. Most patients will find you on Google or your website from a phone, often outside office hours.
  • The right appointment type. A new patient exam, a cleaning, a whitening session, and an emergency visit are not interchangeable — patients want to book the thing they need, with the time it actually takes.
  • Insurance handled up front. Patients want to know whether you take their plan before they book.

What Makes Dental Online Booking Different

Dental booking looks simple on the surface — a patient picks a time — but the schedule underneath has five things a basic booking tool does not handle:

  • Appointments vary widely in length and provider. A visit can be a 30-minute hygiene check, a 60-minute new patient exam, or a two-hour crown prep — and which provider does it matters. The system has to know the duration and provider for each appointment type, not just offer “a slot.”
  • The schedule is per provider and per operatory. A dentist and a hygienist can both be free at 10am, but with one free operatory, only one can see a patient. A tool that checks only the provider’s calendar risks double-booking the chair.
  • Insurance is part of the booking. A practice that books a patient whose plan it does not accept has created a cancellation or a patient who never comes back.
  • New and existing patients need different flows. A new patient needs a longer appointment, intake forms, and insurance details. An existing patient booking a cleaning needs almost nothing.
  • The data is health information. A dental booking can hold a reason for the visit, insurance details, and health history — information under HIPAA. That changes which tools you can use and how reminders and forms must be handled.

The three routes to online booking

Most practices should try them in this order.

Route 1: The scheduling inside your practice management system

The cheapest real option is usually the one you already pay for — or an add-on from the same vendor.

  • Open Dental has Web Sched: New Patient, Existing Patient, Recall, and an ASAP list for filling cancellations. Bookings write into Open Dental automatically, and carrier rules can block plans you do not accept. The price is published: $75 a month per location, or $165 a month for the full eServices bundle.
  • Dentrix (Henry Schein One) and Eaglesoft (Patterson) offer online booking through add-ons and partner tools — Patient Engage and Lighthouse 360 for Dentrix, Weave or RevenueWell for Eaglesoft. The scheduling itself is solid, but the patient-facing booking comes from the add-on, not the base system.

The advantage is the sync: the booking tool and the schedule are the same system. The disadvantage is the patient experience, and on Dentrix and Eaglesoft the add-on price is not published.

Route 2: A dedicated dental scheduling tool

These tools sit between your website and your PMS, and their whole job is to make booking easy for patients while keeping the schedule accurate.

  • NexHealth offers real-time, two-way sync with Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental: a patient books online and the appointment appears in your PMS immediately, and time blocked in the PMS disappears online at once. It also covers forms, reminders, reviews, and payments. It does not publish prices; practices report paying from about $350 a month, plus a reported one-time implementation fee.
  • LocalMed does one job: real-time online scheduling, with a booking button for your website, Google listing, and social media. It syncs with Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, and others, and includes insurance verification. Pricing is quote-only.
  • Weave bundles phones, two-way texting, reminders, reviews, and payments, with scheduling as one part. Published pricing starts at $199 a month per location, but practices commonly report paying $400–$700 a month for the tiers they actually use.
  • Zocdoc is a marketplace where patients search for dentists by location, insurance, and availability — a new-patient channel, not a scheduling system for your existing patients. The basic profile is free; on the Growth plan you pay a fee each time a new patient books — the dental booking fee is reported at about $110 per booking — and the fee can apply even if the patient later cancels. An Advanced tier adds $250 per provider per month.

Route 3: A custom booking page or system

The third option is a booking system built for your practice — a booking page on your own website that connects to your PMS, with your appointment types, your rules, and your branding, and no per-provider or per-location fee that grows with the practice.

A custom build is not the right answer for most single-location practices: if your PMS or a dedicated tool covers what you need, the off-the-shelf option is cheaper and faster. A custom system earns its place when the standard tools do not fit — booking rules the platforms cannot express, a multi-location setup where per-location fees add up, or a practice that wants to own the booking experience and the patient data rather than rent it from a platform. Step-by-step: setting up online booking

Whichever route you choose, the setup follows the same eight steps. Steps 1–4 decide whether the system works; steps 5–8 decide whether patients actually use it.

Step 1: Pick the route — and check the sync first

Before comparing features, confirm that the tool writes appointments into your PMS in real time, in both directions. Ask the vendor, in writing: does a booking made online appear in your PMS immediately, with the right provider, operatory, and appointment type — and does time blocked in the PMS disappear online at once? If the system only syncs every few hours, it creates a significant risk of stale availability and double-booking.

Step 2: Connect the calendar and set who can book what

Connect the tool to your PMS calendar, then decide which appointment types patients can book online at all. A good starting set: new patient exam, cleaning / hygiene recall, and one or two common treatments. Everything else — crown preps, root canals, orthodontic work — stays phone-only until the system has proved itself.

Step 3: Set appointment types and durations honestly

Each appointment type needs its real duration, including the time nobody books: room turnover and charting. A cleaning that takes 45 minutes of chair time plus 15 minutes of turnover is a 60-minute appointment type.

Step 4: Set the booking rules

Step 5: Add forms and insurance

Put the intake forms online and send them with the booking confirmation. Ask for insurance details at booking time and block carriers you do not accept.

Step 6: Set up reminders and confirmations

Three touches work for most practices: a confirmation when the patient books, a reminder 48 hours before, and a reminder the day before — each with a one-tap way to confirm, reschedule, or cancel. If your tool has an ASAP or waitlist feature, switch it on — it is the cheapest way to fill a cancellation.

Step 7: Test it like a patient

Before you tell anyone the booking page exists, book through it yourself: as a new patient, as an existing patient, on a phone, and on a desktop. Check that each booking lands in the PMS correctly, that the reminders arrive, and that the reschedule link works.

Step 8: Launch, link it everywhere, and measure

Put the booking link where patients look: your website header, your Google Business Profile, and your social media profiles. Tell existing patients it exists — a text or email to your recall list is enough. Then measure three numbers in the first month: appointments booked online, show-ups, and bookings the front desk had to fix.

HIPAA: what to get right before you launch

Online booking handles health information, so the setup has a compliance layer a salon booking page does not:

  • Get a signed BAA with every vendor that touches patient data. A Business Associate Agreement defines how a vendor handles Protected Health Information (PHI) on your behalf — what it can use it for, how it protects it, and what happens in a breach. If the vendor will handle PHI and will not sign a BAA, do not put patient data into the system.
  • Do not assume a popular scheduler is fine for patient data. Some widely used scheduling tools are not designed for HIPAA-regulated use and will not sign a BAA at any price. We covered this in our guide to whether Calendly is HIPAA compliant.
  • Keep PHI out of reminders. “You have an appointment tomorrow at 10:00 AM” is very different from a message naming a procedure or a condition. Keep reminder texts to the time, the place, and a link.
  • Use secure forms for intake and insurance. Health history and insurance details should travel through the booking platform’s secure forms, not by email or unencrypted web form.
  • There is no government “HIPAA certified” badge. What you can check is the BAA, the security practices (encryption, access controls, audit logs), and how the vendor handles a breach.

HIPAA compliance is a property of your whole workflow — people, processes, and technology — not a feature of one piece of software.

Five common mistakes

  1. Showing all of your availability. Patients will book the slots you least want filled — the first of the day, the last before lunch, the emergency slot you kept free. Show the openings you want filled and keep the rest phone-only.
  2. Letting patients book any appointment type. A patient who books a 30-minute cleaning slot for a toothache has cost you a slot and a phone call. Start with a small set of appointment types.
  3. Skipping insurance verification. Booking a patient whose plan you do not accept creates a cancellation or a bill the patient did not expect. Check the carrier at booking time.
  4. Ignoring abandoned bookings. A patient who starts booking and stops halfway hit a problem — a confusing form, a missing appointment type, a page that did not load on their phone.
  5. Two calendars. If online bookings do not write into your PMS in real time, your front desk is the integration between two schedules. That is how double-bookings happen.

What it costs

  • PMS built-in booking is usually the cheapest real option. Open Dental’s Web Sched publishes its price at $75 a month per location. Dentrix and Eaglesoft add-ons are quote-only, so get the number in writing before you budget.
  • Dedicated dental appointment scheduling software is a monthly subscription on top of your PMS. NexHealth is reported from about $350 a month, LocalMed is quote-only, and Weave’s published pricing starts at $199 a month per location — with practices commonly reporting $400–$700 a month for the tiers they use, plus a reported ~$750 setup fee.
  • Zocdoc charges per new patient booked, reported at about $110 per dental booking, with no monthly platform fee on the Growth plan. Set a monthly cap and track how many booked patients actually arrive.
  • A custom booking system has an upfront build cost and ongoing maintenance instead of a monthly subscription. It is quoted per project after a free call — for most single-location practices an existing tool is cheaper, and we will say so on the call.

Whatever route you choose, get the full twelve-month total in writing before you sign: subscription, setup, add-ons, and the contract term. Several of these vendors sell on annual contracts with auto-renewal, and the headline monthly price is rarely the whole bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I set up online booking for my dental practice?

Start with your practice management system: check whether Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental already offers online booking or an add-on for it. If the built-in option is not good enough, add a dedicated scheduling tool (NexHealth, LocalMed, or Weave) that syncs with your PMS in real time. Then set your appointment types, booking rules, forms, and reminders — and test the flow before you launch.

Do I need a separate tool, or is my PMS enough?

It depends on your PMS and your patients. Open Dental’s Web Sched is a complete online booking option on its own, and Dentrix and Eaglesoft both offer booking through add-ons. A dedicated tool is worth it when the PMS booking page is hard for patients to use, when you want booking from Google and social media, or when you want features your PMS does not offer.

Will online booking double-book my schedule?

It should not, if the tool syncs with your PMS in real time and in both directions: a booking made online must appear in your PMS immediately, and time blocked in your PMS must disappear from online availability at once. Tools that sync only periodically can create stale availability and increase the risk of double-booking. Ask the vendor to confirm real-time sync in writing.

Is online booking HIPAA compliant?

The booking tool is one part of a HIPAA-compliant workflow, not the whole of it. What the tool must provide is a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) covering the patient data it handles, plus reasonable security practices — encryption, access controls, and audit logs. Our guide to whether Calendly is HIPAA compliant explains the BAA question in detail.

How much does dental online booking cost?

It depends on the route. Open Dental’s Web Sched is published at $75 a month per location. NexHealth is reported from about $350 a month, LocalMed publishes no price, and Weave’s published pricing starts at $199 a month per location, with practices commonly reporting $400–$700 a month. Zocdoc charges per new patient booked, reported at about $110 per dental booking. A custom booking system is quoted per project.

Final Takeaway

Online booking for a dental practice has four parts: a booking page patients can use on a phone, a real-time link to your PMS, booking rules that protect your schedule, and reminders that let patients confirm or move their appointment. Get those four right and the tool almost chooses itself: start with your PMS, add a dedicated tool if the experience or the sync falls short, and build custom only when the standard tools cannot fit how your practice works.

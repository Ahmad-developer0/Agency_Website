Is Calendly HIPAA Compliant? What US Clinics Must Know Before Choosing a Scheduler

By Nexyra Studio · November 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: no. As of November 2026, Calendly does not sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) on any plan — not Free, not Standard, not Teams, not Enterprise — and its own terms tell customers not to put protected health information (PHI) into it. For a US clinic, dental office, or therapy practice, that takes the most popular scheduler on the market off the table on day one.

This guide explains why the BAA is the deciding line, what the alternatives actually cost, and how to choose a scheduling tool without overpaying — or putting patient data somewhere it legally cannot go.

Why a booking form is a HIPAA question

Every online booking form at a medical or dental practice collects protected health information the moment a patient types in a name, a phone number, and a reason for the visit. That makes the scheduling tool more than a calendar — it is software that handles PHI, and HIPAA requires a signed BAA with any vendor that touches it. No BAA, no PHI, no matter how good the encryption sounds in the marketing.

There is also no government body that certifies software as “HIPAA compliant.” No badge, no inspection, no certificate. Compliance is a property of the contract you sign and how you configure the tool — which is why a platform can hold strong security certifications and still be illegal to use for patient data.

What the main schedulers actually do

Before you compare features or prices, ask each vendor one question and get the answer in writing: “Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement?” Here is how the tools clinics actually compare stack up:

  • Calendly — No BAA on any plan. Terms say not to put PHI in it. Free to $16 per seat per month, but not usable for patient data.
  • Acuity Scheduling — Signs a BAA, but only on its top Powerhouse tier: $49 per month billed annually ($61 month-to-month). Flat fee, covers up to 36 calendars.
  • Bookafy — Signs a BAA on Pro+ at $11 per user per month, billed annually. The cheapest HIPAA-capable tier per user in this list.
  • Setmore (Setmore Health) — Signs a BAA on Pro at $5 per user per month, billed annually. The BAA is accepted through the terms of service rather than a separately negotiated document.
  • SimplePractice — Signs a BAA on all plans, from about $39 per month. Scheduling plus telehealth and billing, built for therapists and small clinics.
  • NexHealth — Signs a BAA as part of onboarding, from about $300 per month. The premium option, with real-time sync into Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental.

Prices checked against vendor pages in November 2026. Prices and BAA terms change — confirm both in writing before you commit.

The math changes with practice size

Acuity charges a flat fee while Bookafy and Setmore charge per user, so the cheapest option depends on headcount. A solo provider pays $5 to $11 a month with Setmore or Bookafy, but $49 with Acuity. A 10-provider clinic pays $49 flat with Acuity, versus $50 to $110 a month per-user elsewhere. Work out your number before you compare headline prices.

A scheduler is not a medical records system

Booking, reminders, and visit history are one job. Charting, e-prescribing, insurance claims, and clinical recall are another — that is your EHR or dental practice-management system: Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Epic, Athenahealth. A scheduler will never do those things, and buying one expecting it to is the most common expensive mistake in this category.

If your bottleneck is the phone — new patients who cannot reach you, no-shows, unfilled chairs — a scheduling layer running alongside your records system is the right buy, at a fraction of the cost of a full platform.

How to choose in four steps

  • Ask for the BAA in writing before you trial anything. A “HIPAA compliant” badge on a pricing page is not a legal document.
  • Count your providers and do the per-user vs flat-fee math. The cheapest headline price is rarely the cheapest at your size.
  • Check what reminders cost. SMS reminders are often a paid add-on. Some plans include them; some charge per message.
  • Check what it does not do. No charting, no claims, no PMS sync? Know that before you buy, not after.

When off-the-shelf does not fit

If your scheduling rules are unusual, you run several locations on one patient file, or per-user fees have become a real monthly line, a custom-built scheduling layer can cost less over two years than subscriptions — and the patient data stays in your own database. Frequently asked questions

Is Calendly HIPAA compliant?

No. As of November 2026, Calendly does not sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) on any plan, and its own terms tell customers not to put protected health information into it. Clinics need a scheduler that signs a BAA.

What is a BAA?

A Business Associate Agreement is the contract HIPAA requires between a covered entity, such as your practice, and any vendor that handles protected health information. Without a signed BAA, you cannot legally put patient data into the tool.

Can I use Google Calendar for patient scheduling?

Google Workspace offers a BAA covering its included services, but a personal Google account does not. The BAA question applies to every tool that touches patient data, not just schedulers.

How much does HIPAA-compliant scheduling cost?

From about $5 per user per month to over $300 per month, depending on the tool and the size of your practice. For most small practices, $11 to $49 a month covers a compliant scheduler. Confirm current prices and BAA terms with the vendor before committing.

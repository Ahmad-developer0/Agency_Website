Booking Software for Salons and Barbershops

A salon's whole business is a calendar. Every empty chair-hour is money that cannot be earned back, and every no-show on a Saturday afternoon costs far more than the same slot on a Tuesday morning. Most salons still run on text messages and a diary, which means bookings get missed overnight and nobody can see at a glance who is free.

Booking that matches how a salon actually runs

  • Stylist-wise slots. Clients who always go to one person can pick them directly. Those who do not care get whoever is free, which keeps the chairs full.
  • Real service durations. A trim is not a color. Each service carries its own length and price, so a 20-minute booking cannot eat a two-hour slot.
  • Multiple services in one visit. Cut plus color plus blow-dry adds up to one correctly sized appointment, not three guesses.
  • Buffers and breaks. Clean-up time between clients and staff breaks are respected automatically.
  • Bridal and long bookings. Multi-hour or multi-person bookings get handled properly instead of blocking the whole day by hand.

The no-show problem

Salons feel no-shows harder than most businesses, because the slot was reserved against a specific person's time. Three things reduce them, in this order:

  • A reminder the day before, by text, with a one-tap reschedule. A client who moves the appointment is still revenue; one who disappears is not.
  • A deposit at booking, even a small one, taken through Stripe or Square. This is the single most effective step, and it works best on weekend peak hours and long services where the cost of a gap is highest.
  • A record of who repeatedly does not turn up, so the desk can ask those clients for advance payment while everyone else books normally.

Knowing your clients

Once bookings run through one system, each client builds a history — which stylist, which services, what color formula was used, what they spent, when they last came in. The desk can greet a returning client properly instead of starting from scratch, and the system can tell you who has not visited in three months so you can reach out rather than hope.

That list is the cheapest marketing a salon has. A message to fifty clients who are overdue costs almost nothing and fills more chairs than most paid advertising.

Packages, memberships and loyalty

If you sell session packages, monthly memberships or a loyalty card, the system tracks balances properly: how many sessions remain, when a membership expires, what a client has earned. Done on paper, this leaks money in both directions — sessions given away twice, or regulars who feel short-changed.

Off-the-shelf or custom?

Several salon platforms already exist — Fresha, Vagaro, Booksy, GlossGenius, Square Appointments — and for a single-location salon with standard services, one of them configured well is usually the right answer — cheaper and quicker than building. We will tell you that rather than sell you a build.

Custom is worth it when your setup doesn't fit the box: several locations sharing clients, a product-sales side that must join up with services, pricing rules the platforms can't express, or a team large enough that per-stylist monthly fees — plus marketplace commissions of 20–30% on new clients — have become a real line in your costs. A custom build has no per-seat fee, no marketplace commission, and the client list stays yours.

Keeping it simple for your team

Salon staff are not office workers and the system cannot assume they will be. The day-to-day screen is a calendar, booking takes a few taps, and it works on the phone at the desk. We train your team in one short session and leave a one-page guide. If the software is harder than the diary, it will not be used — so it must not be.

Frequently asked questions

Can clients book through Instagram or Google?

Yes. The booking page is a normal link, so it works in your Instagram bio, a text message, or a Google listing. Most salon bookings start on Instagram or Google, so those links are usually the most valuable ones.

Can we take a deposit before confirming?

Yes, through Stripe or Square, and you can require it only for certain services or peak times rather than for everyone.

What if a stylist leaves or a new one joins?

You add or remove staff yourself from the admin screen, along with their services and working hours. It is not something you need us for.

Will it work for more than one location?

Yes. Each location has its own staff and calendar, while the client list is shared, so a regular who visits another location is recognized.

Do we have to stop taking phone bookings?

No. The desk enters phone and walk-in bookings into the same calendar. Online booking adds a channel, it does not replace the ones you have.

Is it better than the salon software already available?

For a standard single-location salon, often not — an existing platform configured properly is cheaper and faster, and we will say so. Custom pays off with several locations, unusual pricing, or a team big enough that per-seat fees add up.
